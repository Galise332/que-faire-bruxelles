Saint Nicholas and then Christmas are coming, and in Brussels you choose your chocolate in a shop, not on a supermarket shelf. Three very different houses, each with its own story, where you can also sit down for a while.

## 1. Wittamer

**Place du Grand Sablon 12, 1000 Bruxelles** · [wittamer.com](https://www.wittamer.com/)

The big house on the Sablon, with its pink awning and its terrace on the cobblestones of the square. You'll find handmade chocolates, fine pastries, viennoiseries, filled sandwiches and, depending on the season, ice creams and sorbets. Open every day: Monday from 8:30 am to 5:30 pm, Tuesday to Friday from 7:30 am to 6:30 pm, Saturday and Sunday from 7 am to 6:30 pm. Handy for an afternoon treat after a stroll around the Sablon.

## 2. Mary

**Rue Royale 73, 1000 Bruxelles** · [mary.be](https://www.mary.be/fr)

This is where Mary Delluc opened her first shop in 1919, which later became an Art Deco tea room. The house has been a supplier to the Belgian Court since 1942 and takes as much care over its boxes as over its chocolates. The rue Royale shop is open Monday to Saturday from 10 am to 6 pm, closed on Sundays and public holidays. Mary also has shops on the Grand-Place, on rue du Lombard and in the Galerie de la Toison d'Or.

## 3. Frederic Blondeel

**Rue de Ganshoren 39, 1081 Koekelberg** · [frederic-blondeel.be](https://frederic-blondeel.be/)

From three generations of coffee roasters, Frederic Blondeel has been roasting his own cocoa beans for more than thirty years. In Koekelberg, everything is under one roof: the workshop, the shop and a bar where you can have a coffee, a Japanese tea or the house hot chocolate. At the ice cream counter, the flavours are handmade by Frederic himself. Open Monday to Saturday from 9:30 am to 6:30 pm and Sunday from 12 pm to 6 pm, public holidays included.
