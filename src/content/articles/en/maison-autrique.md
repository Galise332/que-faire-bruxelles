In 1893, Victor Horta is 32 when his friend, the engineer Eugène Autrique, asks him to build a house in Schaerbeek. The brief fits in a few words: "no luxury, no extravagance". Yet it became the architect's first town house and a milestone of Art Nouveau, now open to the public on Chaussée de Haecht.

## What to do there

You explore the house at your own pace, from the basement kitchen to the upper floors, in a staging created by Benoît Peeters and François Schuiten. Architect Francis Metzger led the restoration like an archaeologist, removing the paint layer by layer. You can see the result on site: small "windows" left on the walls, doors and skirting boards reveal the old layers. Only the original light points were kept, to bring back the dim atmosphere of the period.

Your ticket also covers the temporary exhibition. Until 7 March 2027, "Maisons Bulles" shows how comic book authors, including Judith Vanistendael and François Schuiten, take on the theme of the house. A small themed bookshop rounds off the visit.

Another way to discover the place: a 2-hour escape game drawn from the world of The Obscure Cities (Les Cités obscures), in which you take over the investigation of a missing researcher.

## Who it's for

Lovers of architecture and Art Nouveau, who see Horta's beginnings here. Comics readers. Families too: the activity booklet "La Maison de Fernand" is for children from age 6 (€3 at the front desk or free to download), and the free "Le Jardin d'Eugène" kit is for ages 7 to 12.

## Practical information

- **Opening hours**: Wednesday to Sunday, 12pm to 6pm, last admission at 5:30pm. Closed on Mondays, Tuesdays and public holidays.
- **Prices**: €9. Seniors, students, teachers and jobseekers: €6. Children aged 6 to 18 and Schaerbeek residents: €3. Free for under-6s and with the museumPASSmusées, Brussels Card or Art Nouveau Pass.
- **Booking**: not needed for individual visits. Guided tours by reservation, at least 2 weeks in advance.
- **Escape game**: 2 to 4 people, recommended from age 16, €12 per person (€10 reduced rate), last start at 3:30pm. It is best to let the museum know beforehand.
- **Getting there**: tram 25 and 62 (Robiano), 92 (Saint-Servais), 93 (Liedts), bus 65 and 66 (Robiano), 59 (Herman). Brussels-North station is a 16-minute walk.
- **Accessibility**: the house is not accessible to people with reduced mobility. A virtual tour is available on the website.
