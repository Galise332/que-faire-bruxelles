At the top of the Mont des Arts, an iron and glass façade catches the eye: this is the former Old England department store, built in 1899 by the architect Paul Saintenoy and described by the museum as one of the finest examples of Art Nouveau architecture in Brussels. Since June 2000, it has been home to the MIM, the Musical Instruments Museum. The museum itself is much older: it was founded in February 1877 and its collection numbers nearly 10,000 instruments.

## What to do there

The visit covers four exhibition floors. The appeal is that you don't just look at instruments behind glass: a multimedia guide, included in the admission price, accompanies you along the way. You can also bring your own headphones (3.5 mm mini-jack).

The museum recommends allowing about two hours. Photos are allowed in the galleries, without flash. Lockers are available, and large bags, backpacks and umbrellas go to the cloakroom.

The MIM also has a concert hall: check its website for the programme. The top-floor restaurant, which offers a view over Brussels, is currently closed for renovation.

## Who it's for

Families, since admission is free up to and including age 17. Music lovers, of course, but also those who enjoy architecture and want to see the inside of a great Art Nouveau building. It's also a good rainy-day outing, a stone's throw from the Gare Centrale. The building is accessible to people with reduced mobility (ramp, lift, adapted toilets, folding seats).
