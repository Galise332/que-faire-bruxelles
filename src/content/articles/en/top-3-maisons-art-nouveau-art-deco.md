Brussels is famous for its Art Nouveau façades, but most of the time you only see them from the pavement. These three houses can be visited inside: two Art Nouveau gems from the early 20th century and an Art Deco villa from the 1930s. Book online before you go.

## 1. Maison Hannon

**Avenue de la Jonction 1, 1060 Saint-Gilles** · [maisonhannon.be](https://maisonhannon.be/fr)

An Art Nouveau masterpiece, designed for Marie Debard and Édouard Hannon by their friend, the architect Jules Brunfaut. On the first floor, you learn about the house's eventful history, from abandonment to restoration. Self-guided visits on Thursday from 1pm to 6pm, Friday from 11am to 6pm, and Saturday and Sunday from 10am to 6pm (closed Monday to Wednesday). €14, €5 for ages 12 to 26, free under 12. Booking online is strongly recommended, and your ticket is valid for the whole day. There are stairs inside. Tram 92 (Ma Campagne or Darwin), tram 4 (Albert), bus 54 (Ma Campagne).

## 2. Maison Cauchie

**Rue des Francs 5, 1040 Etterbeek** · [cauchie.be](https://cauchie.be/)

The private home of the artist Paul Cauchie, built in 1905, which the historian Franco Borsi counted among the finest Art Nouveau works in Brussels. It regularly opens at weekends for guided tours, bookable online via the website. €9.50, €7.50 for students under 26, free for under-12s. Metro Merode (lines 1 and 5), tram 81, buses 27, 61 and 80.

## 3. Villa Empain

**Avenue Franklin Roosevelt 67, 1050 Brussels** · [villaempain.com](https://villaempain.com/votre-visite/)

An Art Deco villa built in the early 1930s by the architect Michel Polak for Baron Louis Empain. In turn a private home, a museum, an embassy and the headquarters of a TV channel, it was restored by the Boghossian Foundation, which holds exhibitions there. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm. €15, €5 for students under 26. The foundation also runs activities for children. Accessible to people with reduced mobility. Trams 8 and 25 (Solbosch), bus 71.
