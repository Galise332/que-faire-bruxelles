In Schaerbeek, a few minutes from Botanique metro station, a large building "dressed all in glass and steel" hosts dance, circus and concerts. This is the Halles de Schaerbeek, a former covered market turned into a performance venue more than fifty years ago. People come here to see artists they won't find anywhere else, in a setting that has kept its metal beams and glass roofs.

## What to do here

The programme revolves around three disciplines: dance and performance, circus, and music, along with stand-up and cabaret. Autumn is mostly devoted to the performing arts; from February to May, music takes over. At the end of the season, the Halles hold a "Grand Marché".

The history of the place can be read in its walls. The Sainte-Marie covered market was built in 1865, burned down in 1898, then rose again in 1901. Abandoned in 1920, it served as a municipal depot and garage for half a century. In 1973-1974, a collective led by Jo Dekmine set up an independent cultural centre there. The Grande Halle was reopened at the end of 1996 after renovation, and the Halles celebrated their 50th anniversary in 2024.

The bar welcomes the audience an hour before and an hour after each show: handy for arriving early and making the evening last.

## Who it's for

For curious minds who like to be surprised: contemporary dance audiences, circus lovers, fans of concerts in a hall with striking architecture. The pay-what-you-can system also makes it a good spot for students and tight budgets. The programme changes from one evening to the next: check each show's page before you go.

## Practical information

- **Address**: rue Royale Sainte-Marie 22a, 1030 Schaerbeek
- **Box office**: Wednesdays from 2 pm to 6 pm and one hour before each performance; bookings at reservation@halles.be or on 02 218 21 07
- **Prices**: "Pay What You Can", five prices to choose from for each show, including a recommended median price. Student and association rate usually €10 or €12, solidarity tickets at €5. Partner of Article 27. €1 fee for online purchases
- **Getting there**: trams 25 and 62 and buses 65-66, Robiano stop; metro 2 and 6, Botanique stop; Brussels-North station 500 m away
- **By car**: no public car park nearby, better to come by public transport or by bike (bike racks opposite the building)
