It's easy to walk past without noticing: in the Galerie de la Reine, an entrance of tall curved shop windows, a ticket booth on one side and the words « Cinéma Galeries » in gold letters. Behind it lies an arthouse cinema built in 1939, still owned by the same family company, with two screens, a bar and a 1,000 m² exhibition hall. A great idea for an evening in the city centre, a short walk from the Grand-Place.

## What to do there

You come for the films first. The programme ranges from the latest arthouse releases to retrospectives of great independent directors, with previews, film series and festivals throughout the year.

The building is worth the visit in itself. It was designed by architect Paul Bonduelle for the brothers Oswald and Robert Putzeys, in a style between Art Deco and Modernism, with decoration that has a Mediterranean touch. The original single 600-seat auditorium was split in 1973: the second screen was built into the former balcony, which you can still recognise today. The cinema has been a listed monument since 1993, and its entrance was restored identically in 2005-2006.

The large exhibition hall hosts temporary exhibitions: the current programme is on the website.

## Who it's for

Film lovers looking for something other than the big blockbusters, a first date or a night out with friends in the centre. Families are not forgotten: an « En famille » section gathers films for young audiences, and the Filem'On festival, which has been showing films for children and teenagers for 20 years, takes place there from 26 October to 7 November 2026.

## Practical info

- **Address**: Galerie de la Reine 28, 1000 Brussels (Royal Saint-Hubert Galleries).
- **Opening hours**: the cinema opens 30 minutes before the first screening of the day and closes 30 minutes after the last one. Showtimes are on the website.
- **Prices**: €10.20 full price, €7.80 reduced (students, over-65s, teachers, jobseekers… with proof), €7.00 for 18 and under, €6.50 for 12 and under.
- **Tickets**: online on the website or at the cinema. Booking is not required; tickets bought online are non-refundable.
- **Getting there**: metro Gare Centrale or De Brouckère, bus 29 and 71 (Arenberg stop) or 38, 63 and 66 (Gare Centrale stop), Brussels-Central station on foot.
