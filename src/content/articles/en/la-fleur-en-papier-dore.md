Push open the door of La Fleur en Papier Doré and you step into a café where nothing has been redone to look pretty. The walls are covered with drawings, hand-painted sayings, frames and objects piled up since the 1940s. The building has been listed since 1997, and you visit it the way you visit a small museum, except that here you sit down and order something.

## What you do there

You come first for the atmosphere: worn wooden tables, an old stove, benches, and the feeling of settling into a living room that has seen a lot. Since spring 2023 the house has closed its kitchen to become a pure café again, a place to meet, read and talk. It describes itself as a "cultural estaminet" and hosts meetings, readings, concerts and talks, with the programme published in the website's agenda. The two rooms can also be hired.

History is part of the decor. Geert Van Bruaene, gallery owner and poet, moved in here in 1944 and stayed until 1964, surrounded by his artist friends. Magritte and the Brussels surrealists were regulars at these tables, then the CoBrA group with Dotremont, Alechinsky and Pol Bury. Hugo Claus celebrated his first wedding here. After the bankruptcy of 2006, a group of enthusiasts bought the café back as a cooperative, 128 members in all, and reopened it on 12 October 2007 after fourteen months of work.

## Who it's for

The curious who want to see a genuine pre-war Brussels café with no staging, art lovers who know Magritte and CoBrA, and anyone looking for a quiet spot for a coffee during the day or a calm evening a stone's throw from the Sablon and the Marolles.

## Practical information

- **Hours**: open every day until midnight. The website gives a morning opening (8am or 10am depending on the page): check before going early.
- **Kitchen**: closed since late May 2023, it is a café only.
- **Events**: full programme in the official website's agenda.
- **Premetro**: Anneessens (trams 3 and 4) is a 5-minute walk; Central Station about ten minutes.
