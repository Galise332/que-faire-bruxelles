You can cross the Place Royale without suspecting a thing. Yet just beneath the cobblestones lie the cellars of one of the largest palaces in Europe. From the 12th to the 18th century, the Palais du Coudenberg looked down over Brussels from its hill: the Dukes of Burgundy lived there, then Emperor Charles V, then the Archdukes Albert and Isabella. In 1731 a fire destroyed it almost entirely, and its ruins were cleared in the 1770s to build today's Place Royale. What survived can now be visited underground.

## What to do there

You head down from the reception of the BELvue museum and follow a route through underground rooms and passageways. You pass through the cellars of the main residence, the oldest part of the site, which lay beneath the princes' apartments. You cross the foundations of the chapel built under Charles V, then the cellar level of the Aula Magna, the great ceremonial hall where the emperor abdicated in 1555. The most striking moment: the rue Isabelle, a real medieval street once open to the sky, now under a vault. The route ends in the fully restored Gothic gallery of the hôtel d'Hoogstraeten.

The site has chosen to keep signage to a minimum. For explanations, there is a free audio guide to listen to on your phone, in French, Dutch, English, German or Ukrainian: just follow the red numbers from 1 to 39. There is no 4G underground, but free Wi-Fi is available at the turnstiles.

## Who it's for

Families with children who love castles and underground passages: admission is free for under-18s. History buffs who want to understand why the Royal Quarter looks the way it does. And anyone looking for an indoor outing on a rainy day, a stone's throw from the Mont des Arts.

## Practical information

- **Opening hours**: Tuesday to Friday from 9.30am to 5pm; weekends and in July-August from 10am to 6pm. Closed on 1 January, 21 July and 25 December; closes at 4pm on 24 and 31 December.
- **Duration**: about 1 to 1.5 hours.
- **Prices**: €10 adults, €8 seniors (65+), €5 for 18-25s, free for under-18s. Combined ticket with the BELvue museum: €18. Free with the Brussels Card and the museumPASSmusées.
- **Tickets**: from the BELvue museum's online shop or on site.
- **Getting there**: shared entrance with the BELvue museum. Central Station is a 7-minute walk away; metro Parc (lines 1 and 5) or Trône (lines 2 and 6); trams 92 and 93.
- **Accessibility**: two lifts, but only part of the site is wheelchair accessible (not the Aula Magna or the upper part of the rue Isabelle). The floor is uneven in places.
