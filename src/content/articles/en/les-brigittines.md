At the foot of the Chapelle church, where the Sablon slopes down towards the Marolles, Les Brigittines serves food that proudly owns its Brussels and Belgian roots. In the kitchen is Dirk Myny, a master chef who describes his table as "la cuisine plaisir". No passing fads here: the menu follows the seasons and the country's classics, prepared with care.

## What to eat

The menu posted on the restaurant's website at the time of writing sets the tone. To start, grey shrimp croquettes, a langoustine bisque with black pudding, or scallops meunière with green cabbage. For mains, moules marinière, a seasonal vol-au-vent, veal cheek braised for four hours, and in autumn, game: roast partridge, pheasant crépinette à la brabançonne, saddle of hare to share between two. For dessert, safe bets: crème brûlée with Tahitian vanilla, rice pudding with salted butter caramel, apple millefeuille with raw cream.

At lunchtime, a fixed-price lunch is offered on weekdays; in the evening, a multi-course menu features the dishes of the moment. The à la carte menu is not available on Friday evenings.

## Who it's for

For a quiet business lunch close to the centre, for a dinner where you want to taste Belgian cooking done by the book, or to introduce a visitor to dishes you find less and less often: bouillon, pig's trotter, calf's brains "the way my grandmother made them". A private room for 26 people also hosts group meals.

## Practical info

- **Opening hours**: Monday to Friday, 12pm to 2pm and 7pm to 10pm. Closed at weekends and on public holidays.
- **Prices** (at the time of publication): evening menu at 68,75 €, à la carte mains from 31 € to 52 €, desserts between 12,75 € and 15,75 €.
- **Reservations**: by phone on 02 512 68 91 or by email at info@lesbrigittines.com.
- **Getting there**: Bruxelles-Chapelle station is just a few steps away.
