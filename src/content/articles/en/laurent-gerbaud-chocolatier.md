In the Rue Ravenstein shop, the workshop sits behind a pane of glass, so you can watch the chocolates being made right in front of you. That is Laurent Gerbaud's principle: nothing hidden. This Belgian chocolatier, named Brussels Chocolatier of the Year 2021 by Gault&Millau, runs his shop and tea room steps from the Mont des Arts, and every Saturday he opens his workshop to anyone keen to get their hands in the chocolate.

## What you do there

The introductory workshop, led by Laurent Gerbaud, comes in two parts. First, you put on an apron and create your own mendiants, small rectangles of dark chocolate topped with dried or candied fruit: you mould, unmould, wrap and take them home. Then comes a "palate education" tasting: twelve chocolates to discover certain cocoa varieties, followed by the house classics.

Even without a workshop, the place is worth a visit. The tea room serves homemade hot chocolate with 70% cocoa, or a coffee with a ganache chosen at the counter, and there is ice cream too. The flavour pairings were born after a two-year journey through China: Shanghai orange, Madagascar pepper, Izmir fig and black Taggiasca olive all meet chocolate here.

## Who it's for

Families with children aged 6 and over, friends, colleagues (the house also runs team-building sessions) and visitors passing through, since the workshop is held in French and English. Under-12s must be accompanied by a participating adult, with one adult per child. Companions without a booked place wait in the shop.
