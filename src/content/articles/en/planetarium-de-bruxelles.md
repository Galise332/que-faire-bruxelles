You settle into a seat, the lights dim, and the ceiling turns into the sky. The Planetarium of the Royal Observatory of Belgium, at Heysel, screens its films in 360° on a dome, the only one of its kind in Brussels according to the venue. A trip through space without leaving Laeken, and a great idea when it rains.

## What you do there

You pick a film from the day's programme. There is something for every age: a show for little ones (3 to 7, 30 minutes) that uses fairy tales to introduce the Great Bear, the zodiac and shooting stars, « Terre, Lune, Soleil » and « L'Aveugle aux yeux d'étoiles » for 6 to 12-year-olds, « La Grande aventure du Système Solaire ! » from age 8, then a series of films from age 12 on stars, the Sun, climate, neutrinos or icy worlds. For the little ones' show, headphones let you follow along in French, Dutch or English.

The Planetarium also hosts occasional talks and offers astronomy lessons to schools, from nursery to higher education.

## Who it's for

Families, from age 3 thanks to the dedicated show. Kids who love planets and grown-ups who still like looking up. School classes, with an optional workshop. And anyone spending the day at the Atomium or Mini-Europe next door: combined tickets are available.

## Practical info

- **Opening hours**: ticket desk and reception every day from 10am to 5pm. Film times on the website, arrive 15 minutes early. Closed on 24, 25 and 31 December and 1 January.
- **Prices** (per film): adults €9, young people (3-25) and seniors (65+) €7, jobseekers and social income recipients €3.50. One euro off with an Atomium ticket.
- **Getting there**: metro 6 (Houba-Brugmann), tram 7 (Heysel), 9, 51 and 93 (Stade), bus 14 or 83 (Heysel).
- **Booking**: online ticketing on the website, or +32 2 474 70 50 (Monday to Friday, 9.30am-4.30pm).
