On the Parvis de Saint-Gilles, the Brasserie Verschueren has been open since 1880. Louis-François Verschueren founded it as an outlet for the gueuzes, krieks and fruit liqueurs made by the small family brewery, located right next door on rue de l'Église. The family lived upstairs in this neoclassical building dating from 1870. In 1935, his son Jean called on the architect Bautrix, who gave the brasserie its Art Deco character. Today the place is a listed building, while remaining a lively spot where people meet.

## What to do there

People come here for a coffee, a conversation or some reading. The list of hot drinks is long: coffee, cappuccino, hot chocolate, lait russe, chai latte, and a teapot to choose from around ten teas and herbal infusions. The house also makes its own cold drinks: ginger juice, ginger lemonade, homemade Club-Mate, lemonade and homemade iced tea. For a bite, the menu stays simple (crisps, a portion of cheese, Gaume sausage) and the house points out that you can bring whatever you want to eat.

On the beer side, two recipes bear the house name: a Tripel Verschu, brewed by Brasserie de la Senne, and a Saison Verschu, brewed by Brasserie De Ranke.

Look out for the big match board: it carries plates in the colours of the 128 teams of the top divisions of the 1950s, whose results Roger Verschueren used to chalk up. It reflects the family's bond with Union Saint-Gilloise: Franz Verschueren played in the first team from 1941 to 1954, before going on to run the club. During the Occupation, the brasserie was also one of the bases of the Resistance, and for decades some forty associations held their meetings in the upstairs rooms.

## Who it's for

Neighbourhood residents, for whom it is a meeting point. Anyone curious about Brussels history and Art Deco. Union supporters who want to see where part of the club's history was written. The house displays a clear rule: the Verschu must remain a welcoming, warm and safe place for everyone.
