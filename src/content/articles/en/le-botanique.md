Victor Hugo said Brussels had two marvels: the Grand-Place and the view of the Botanical Garden. The scientific garden moved to Meise in 1939, but the large glass building opened in 1829, designed by the architect Charles-Henri Petersen, stayed put on the edge of the inner ring road. Since 24 January 1984, it has been the cultural centre of the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, and one of the busiest concert venues in the city.

## What to do there

Music comes first. The Bota programmes concerts all year round in several intimate halls, including the Rotonde and the Museum. In the 1990s, Oasis, dEUS, The Smashing Pumpkins and Jeff Buckley played here before going on to fill stadiums. Since 1994, the Nuits Botanique festival has turned the venue into one big music party.

The other half of the programme is visual art: exhibitions open during the day, in the building's spaces. Félicien Rops, Topor, Magritte, Henri Cartier-Bresson and the Magnum agency have all been shown here.

For food, La Cantine du Bota cooks local, seasonal produce, with a vegan selection. On concert nights, it switches to street food.

## Who it's for

Curious music fans who like to discover a band before everyone else, exhibition lovers looking for an afternoon outing, and families, thanks to Bota Kids, a programme that opens up music and the visual arts to younger audiences. Concerts generally finish around 10.30pm, so you can get home by public transport.

## Practical information

- **Exhibitions**: Wednesday to Sunday, 12pm to 6pm. Tickets on sale directly in the exhibition spaces.
- **Concerts**: programme and tickets on botanique.be. Stage times are published on the website at 2pm on the day.
- **La Cantine du Bota**: Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm (until 5pm on concert days); in the evening from 6.30pm to 11.30pm, for concert ticket holders only. Booking for lunch only.
- **Getting there**: Botanique metro (lines 2 and 6), trams 92 and 93, bus 61. A 15-minute walk from Brussels-North station.
- **Contact**: 02 218 37 32.
