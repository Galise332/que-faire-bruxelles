This isn't the big Magritte Museum on Place Royale, it's his home. From 1930 to 1954, René Magritte and his wife Georgette lived on Rue Esseghem in Jette, longer than anywhere else. The painter created about half of his work here, and the Belgian surrealists met here every week. Rediscovered in 1992 by André Garitte, an art enthusiast, the house became a museum that opened its doors on 5 June 1999, after more than six years of work.

## What you'll find

You climb the floors of a 1925 house that has stayed intact. The biographical section tells the story of Magritte's life through archives, photos, letters, objects and around twenty original works: gouaches, drawings and two oil paintings. There are also about ten advertising works, a reminder of Studio Dongo, the small advertising agency he set up here. It was in this house that he came up with paintings that became famous, such as *L'Empire des Lumières* and *Golconde*.

The visit ends in the couple's apartment, restored with the original furniture. The same building houses a Museum of Abstract Art: more than 200 paintings, sculptures and other works, and a second floor that links Belgian abstraction with surrealism. You'll also come across other surrealist artists, such as Paul Delvaux, Jane Graverol, Marcel Mariën and Rachel Baes. Until 24 January 2027, a focus is devoted to Félix Labisse.

## Who it's for

Magritte fans who have already seen Place Royale and want to step into his everyday life. Curious visitors who like artists' houses on a human scale. On the last Wednesday of each month, a guided tour is included in the entry ticket. Note: the building is listed and is not accessible to people with reduced mobility or to pushchairs.
