A black shopfront, two lanterns and, in gold letters, "J. Dandoy 31". The shop on Rue au Beurre, a few steps from the Grand-Place, is the oldest of Maison Dandoy. The story begins in 1829, when baker Jean-Baptiste Dandoy opened his first shop in the heart of Brussels. In 1858 he moved to Rue au Beurre and passed the trade on to his son Philippe. More than 150 years later, the same family still runs the house, now in its seventh generation with Alexandre and Antoine Helson.

## What to eat

The speciality is **speculoos**, butter-and-sugar biscuits that the house makes with "the same old recipes". Around them, the range covers **biscuits**, **breads**, **cakes**, **confectionery** and **waffles**. The house puts the emphasis on fresh, natural ingredients.

One piece of history says a lot about the place: after the Second World War, Valère Rombouts, who was running the company at the time, waited until 1948 to bring back all the specialities, until he could find butter and sugar that met his standards.

Right now, Saint Nicholas is in the spotlight: the assortments can be pre-ordered online and collected in the shop from 27 November.

## Who it's for

For visitors who want to take home a real souvenir of Brussels, something other than a keyring. For families getting ready for Saint Nicholas. And for anyone passing by the Grand-Place who fancies a biscuit with a story.
