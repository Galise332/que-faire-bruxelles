You push open a cast-iron door marked with an A, and the noise of Rue Dansaert stops dead. Inside: art deco curves, original benches and, in the middle of the room, a 1929 grand piano set between two columns. L'Archiduc hasn't changed since 1937, and that is exactly what you come for.

## What you do there

You settle in for a cocktail, the house speciality, somewhere between classic recipes and new creations. But the real draw is the music. On Saturdays, "Jazz After Shopping" from 5pm to 7pm; on Sundays, "Round About Five", at the same hours. Intimate sessions, with established musicians and young talent alike, in a room where the piano is never far from your table.

The history of the place is written on its walls. Madame Alice opened the bar in 1937. In 1953 she handed it over to Stan Brenders, a pioneer of Belgian jazz, founder of the INR jazz orchestra in 1936 and a friend of Nat King Cole, who sat at the piano almost every evening. Since 1985, Jean-Louis Hennart and his wife have kept the place alive and booked the concerts. A book, *L'Archiduc Forever*, tells the whole story.

## Who it's for

Jazz lovers who want to hear a concert without a ticket or a big hall. Couples looking for a calm, beautiful spot after dinner. Architecture buffs: it is one of the best-preserved art deco interiors in the centre.

## Practical info

- **Hours**: open every day from 4pm to 5am. Closed on Christmas Eve.
- **Concerts**: Saturday and Sunday, from 5pm to 7pm.
- **Booking**: none, just walk in and sit down.
- **Premetro**: Bourse stop, a two-minute walk.
