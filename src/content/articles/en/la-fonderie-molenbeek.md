Before it became a city of offices, Brussels was a city of factories. Along the canal in Molenbeek, the neighbourhood was even nicknamed “Little Manchester”. La Fonderie, the Brussels museum of industry and work, tells that story right where it happened: in the buildings of the former Compagnie des Bronzes, a factory abandoned in 1977. The association was founded in 1983 to save this heritage (sites, tools, trades, know-how) and moved onto the site in 1986.

## What to do there

You visit the permanent exhibition *Made in Brussels*. It first shows how the city grew around its industries, then focuses on four sectors that shaped it: metal, of which the factory itself is a direct witness, wood, textile manufacturing and food production. Each section is built around two “star” machines, such as a Jacquard loom, and covers techniques, the history of a company, workers' conditions and home-based work.

Over the decades, La Fonderie has gathered a large collection of objects and machines, archives and oral testimonies. It also offers educational activities, guided tours of the city and a documentation centre.

For a break, Zot, the community canteen on site, serves low-cost meals at tiered or pay-what-you-can prices (kitchen open from 12pm to 2pm).

## Who it's for

For the curious who want to understand where the city they cross every day comes from. For families, since entry is free for children under 12. For schools of the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, which visit for free with teaching materials. And for anyone who loves old machines and places full of history.

- **Prices**: €8; €5 for seniors (65+), students, jobseekers and PROF card holders; free for under-12s and with the Museum Pass. Free for everyone on the first Sunday of the month.
- **Getting there**: metro Comte de Flandre; tram 51 (Porte de Flandre stop) and 82 (Triangle stop); bus 89 (Triangle stop). Brunfaut underground car park for drivers.
