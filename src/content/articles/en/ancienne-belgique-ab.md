In the middle of the Boulevard Anspach pedestrian zone, the Ancienne Belgique, known to everyone as the AB, is one of the best-known concert halls in Brussels. The site has a long history: in the 15th century it was home to the house of the overseas merchants, and a plaque reading "Meersliedenambacht 1781" can still be seen on the façade. In the early 20th century people came here for revues and variety shows, before it became a renowned music hall.

## What to do there

You come for a concert. After the war, Charles Trenet, Édith Piaf, Georges Brassens, Charles Aznavour and Jacques Brel all played the AB stage. In the 1970s the hall welcomed Leonard Cohen, Frank Zappa, Lou Reed and Kraftwerk. Today the programme runs across six spaces: the Main Hall, AB Club, AB Salon, AB Antenna, AB Rooftop and AB Connect. The Salon and the Antenna are at 11 Steenstraat, on the corner of the main entrance.

The exact schedule of each evening is posted on the concert page the day before at the latest. For a regular concert, doors usually open at 7 pm and the evening ends by 10:30 pm, leaving time to get home by public transport.

## Who it's for

For live music lovers, from new discoveries to big names. The reasonable hours also suit people who work the next day or come in by train from another city. Anyone who needs a seat or assistance can request it from the venue.

## Practical info

- **Tickets**: online 24/7 via the website's agenda; box office open Monday to Friday, 10 am to 5 pm. Free waiting list for sold-out shows.
- **Gift**: concert vouchers for any amount you choose, valid for one year.
- **Tram**: 4 and 10, Bourse stop. **Metro**: 1 and 5, De Brouckère stop. The Event Pass QR code on your ticket gets you a free STIB ticket.
- **Train**: Brussels-Central is a 10-minute walk; the SNCB code on your ticket gives 40% off a Bravo! Ticket.
- **Contact**: 02 548 24 84.
