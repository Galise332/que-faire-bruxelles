You go in through a neoclassical pavilion at Porte d'Anderlecht, head a few metres down beneath the boulevard, and come out through the pavilion opposite. In between: the Senne, the river Brussels covered over, and a real sewer. Opened in 1988 and renovated in 2007, the Sewer Museum tells the story of what goes on beneath our feet.

## What to do there

The route follows the city's water, from the tap to the treatment plant. Photos, models, videos and objects show the work of the sewer workers, who inspect, repair and modernise the network every day, and how the sewers protect the city from flooding. Archive photos trace the history of the Senne, open-air at first and then vaulted over in the 19th and 20th centuries.

The highlight is the descent: a walk along the Senne, then about 50 metres on foot through the Chaussée de Mons collector, a large sewer still in use. Rats? The museum explains that they are useful to the network, and that you only spot them from a distance, early in the day.

Until 3 January 2027, the exhibition "Back on Senne" accompanies the visit, with a light and sound piece by Romain Tardy and Coline Cornélis. Allow about an hour on site.

## Who it's for

Families first and foremost: the museum can be fully enjoyed from age 5. A treasure hunt booklet (ages 5-7) and a "Guide de l'exploRATeur" (ages 8-12) are handed out free at the front desk, and a "choose your own adventure" audio guide is available from age 12 (€2). Boots and a torch are optional, but avoid open-toed shoes.

## Practical information

- **Opening hours**: Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm. Closed on Mondays, as well as on 1 January, 1 May, 1 and 11 November and 25 December. Closes at 4pm on 24 and 31 December. Closed from 4 to 8 January 2027 and from 15 to 22 March 2027.
- **Prices**: €10, €7 for over-65s, €5 for students over 18 and residents of the City of Brussels. Free for under-18s, with the MuseumPassMusées or the Brussels Card, and for everyone on the first Sunday of the month.
- **Booking**: recommended, online. Required for groups.
- **Getting there**: tram 51 and 82, bus 46, Porte d'Anderlecht stop. About a 13-minute walk from Brussels-South station.
- **Good to know**: in heavy rain, the Senne and the collector may be closed for safety reasons. You then receive a ticket to come back and see them for free. The building is not wheelchair accessible.
