Two streets from the Bourse, a large brick-and-iron hall fills the middle of Place Saint-Géry. You walk in for a drink under the metal roof structure, and you walk out having seen an exhibition, learned that Brussels is said to have been born right here, and glimpsed the Senne, the river the city covered over almost entirely.

## What to do there

The building was begun in 1881 to plans by architect Adolphe Vanderheggen and opened in 1882. For nearly a century it was a covered market, the "belly of Brussels", until it closed in 1977. Listed since 1987, it is now a "cultural factory" run under the aegis of the Brussels-Capital Region.

On the ground floor, the Marché Saint-Géry came back to life in spring 2026, in the spirit of the original market hall. The bar area is where you settle in: you can have a drink, including beers from the in-house microbrewery, and eat there at weekends, with free wifi.

In the rest of the building, the programme alternates exhibitions, talks, concerts and performances, with the aim of giving everyone free access to culture. Until 10 January 2027, the exhibition "La Belgique émaillée" tells the story of the enamel signs that shaped Belgian streets, alongside works by contemporary artists.

Finally, the information centre offers books and brochures on the architecture and history of Brussels, as well as free guided tours of Place Saint-Géry and its enclosed courtyard, where you can see the last open-air remnant of the Senne in the city centre.

## Who it's for

Anyone looking for a central spot to sit for a while, Brussels history buffs, families who want a free exhibition between two walks, and visitors who want to understand the city beyond the Grand-Place, 500 metres away.
