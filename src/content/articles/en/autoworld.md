Before you even get to the cars, there's the building: a glass and steel hall built for the 1888 World's Fair, right next to the Cinquantenaire arch, with a roof span of 48 metres. This is where the very first motor shows were held between 1902 and 1934. Since 1986, Autoworld has told the story of the car here with more than 300 vehicles, many of them from the Mahy family collection.

## What to do

You travel through time zone by zone. Salon 1910 recreates an early 20th-century motor show with around thirty period cars. Then come the 1920s and 30s, the post-war years and their chrome, and the 60s to 80s, from the Ford Mustang to the Lamborghini Countach. One zone is devoted to Belgian makes such as Minerva and Imperia, another to royal cars, including King Albert I's personal Minerva.

Motorsport fans walk past the banked curve and starting grid of the Sport & Competition zone, where more than 20 racing cars are on display. Comic book fans will find a Michel Vaillant area, where you can pose in a Formula 1 car, and two cars straight out of the Tintin albums. Every year, two major temporary exhibitions and many small pop-up shows keep the visit fresh.

An audio guide is included in the ticket, with a family route. During school holidays, a Kids Corner offers LEGO bricks, colouring and a rally simulator. There's also an escape game played among the cars, by reservation.

## Who it's for

Families, clearly, and car and motorbike enthusiasts. But also anyone who loves architecture and Brussels history: the hall alone is worth the trip. Allow about 1h30 for the visit.

## Practical info

- **Opening hours**: Monday to Friday 10am to 5pm, Saturday and Sunday 10am to 6pm.
- **Prices**: adult €18, students, seniors and people with reduced mobility €14, children 6 to 12 €11, free under 6. Museum Pass and Brussels Card accepted.
- **Escape game**: €30 per game (museum ticket not included), 6 people maximum.
- **Metro**: Merode, the museum is in the Cinquantenaire park.
- **On site**: Brasserie Bagnole, also open to people not visiting the museum, with a view of the collection.
