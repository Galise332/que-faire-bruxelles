A few steps from Manneken Pis, at number 5 Rue du Chêne, the Poechenellekelder has been open since 1991. It calls itself an authentic estaminet, the old Belgian word for a neighbourhood tavern, and that is exactly how it feels when you walk in: dim lighting, walls covered in old photos and engravings, and a mix of locals and tourists. The name comes from Brussels dialect: a ‘poechenelle’ is a puppet, and a ‘kelder’ is a cellar. The house motto sums up the spirit of the place: ‘Au Poech un jour, au Poech toujours !’ (once at the Poech, always at the Poech).

## What to do there

Drop in to catch your breath after a stroll around the Grand-Place, take your time looking at the walls, and chat. The food menu is simple and very Belgian: tartines (open sandwiches) with cheeses from the country (fromage blanc, Brussels cheese, pottekees, mild Herve), tartines with cold cuts such as kip-kap or Ardennes smoked ham, croque-monsieur, spaghetti and homemade lasagne bolognese. To finish, a homemade chocolate cake.

For drinks, the menu runs from ‘real hot chocolate’ to teas, herbal teas, juices and freshly squeezed orange juice, with a long list of Belgian beers for adults. The menu is available as a PDF on the website, in thirteen languages.

## Who it's for

Visitors who want to see an old-fashioned Brussels café without leaving the centre. Locals looking for an easy-to-find meeting point. Families who want a tartine-and-hot-chocolate break between two visits. On sunny days, the terrace lets you enjoy the street.
