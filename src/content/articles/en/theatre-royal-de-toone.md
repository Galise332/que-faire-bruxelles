First you have to find the entrance: a narrow alley opening at number 66 rue du Marché-aux-Herbes, a hundred metres from the Grand-Place. At the far end stands an old Brussels house, with a bar on the ground floor and, upstairs, a room where rod puppets have been performing Dracula, Cyrano or the Nativity for nearly two centuries. The Théâtre royal de Toone describes itself as the only traditional Brussels puppet theatre still in operation.

## What you do there

You come to see a show. The repertoire draws on the great classics (Cyrano, Dracula, the Nativity at Christmas…) adapted for puppets operated from above, in the setting of a small popular theatre. Plays are in French unless stated otherwise, and the house has also adapted shows in English, Spanish, Italian and German; a « Nativitas » is even performed in Brussels Vloms, the city's dialect.

The history is part of the show. Around 1830, Antoine Genty, known as « Toone » (the Brussels diminutive of Antoine), opened his « poechenellekelder ». The title has been passed down ever since: José Géal became Toone VII in 1963, and his son Nicolas Géal was enthroned as Toone VIII on 10 December 2003 at the Hôtel de Ville. The building and the impasse Schuddeveld have been listed together as a protected ensemble since 1997.

During the interval, spectators go up free of charge to the first-floor museum, where the puppets « retired from the stage » are on display. And before or after, the Estaminet de Toone on the ground floor welcomes everyone, spectator or not, with board games freely available.

## Who it's for

Families looking for a short, lively cultural outing (the sessions organised with Article 27 are announced as « for all ages from 7 »), lovers of Brussels heritage, and anyone who wants to show visitors something you won't see anywhere else. Groups and companies can book guided tours and private shows for up to 140 people.

## Practical information

- **Shows**: Thursday, Friday and Saturday at 8.30pm, plus a Saturday performance at 4pm. The programme may change, check the website.
- **Price**: €20 full price (ages 25 to 64), €15 for under-25s and those aged 65 and over. Cash payment just before the show, unnumbered seats.
- **Booking**: recommended, via the website form or on +32 2 513 54 86.
- **Tavern**: noon to midnight, Wednesday to Sunday. Closed Monday and Tuesday.
- **Museum**: free during the interval for spectators. The Maison de la Marionnette is open free of charge Thursday to Saturday, 2pm to 6pm.
- **Getting there**: Gare Centrale or Bourse premetro a few minutes' walk away.
