At the end of Brussels Park, on Rue de la Loi, stands one of the oldest theatres in the city. The Théâtre Royal du Parc was born in 1782, when the Bultos brothers were granted permission to build a "small theatre" there. It became royal in 1816 and has belonged to the City of Brussels since 1819. People still come today for its velvet seats and its large painted dome.

## What to do there

You go there to see theatre, with big stories told for the widest possible audience. That is the line championed by its director, Thierry Debroux: bringing three generations of theatregoers together in the same auditorium. The 2026-2027 season gives a good idea of it: *Robin des Bois* from 5 November to 31 December 2026, followed by *Lady Frankenstein*, *Einstein au Coq* and *La Confusion des sentiments* until June 2027.

The auditorium itself is worth the trip. Its decoration and ceiling date from 1881, under the direction of the architect Pierre Victor Jamaer, who also designed the spiral stone staircase leading up to the top floors. After a fire in 1998, it was restored in its neoclassical style.

Before evening performances, the Bistro du Parc serves salads, tarts and hot dishes prepared by La Maison des Tartes from 7pm, by reservation, at €14 to €16 per dish.

## Who it's for

Families who want to take their children to the theatre for the first time. Grandparents returning to an auditorium they have known for a long time. Under-26s, who often pay half price. History and architecture buffs who want to see a real Italian-style auditorium right in the city centre.
