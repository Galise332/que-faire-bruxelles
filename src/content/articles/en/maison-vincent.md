You come in from a little street a stone's throw from the Galeries Saint-Hubert and find yourself in a room lined entirely with earthenware tiles: on the back wall, a large tiled fresco shows a fishing boat caught in the waves. The house has been here since 1913. Long known as "restaurant Vincent", in 2025 it went back to its original name: Maison Vincent. Jacques Brel was a loyal customer, and the Fondation Jacques Brel has included it in its trail through the city.

## What's on the plate

Homemade Brussels cooking, with plenty of Belgian specialities and meat as the highlight: it is carved and flambéed in the dining room, in front of the guests. The house works with short supply chains and local produce, and it received the Good Food label in 2023. The menu offers vegetarian, vegan, lactose-free and gluten-free options. In season, game suggestions are added. From Monday to Friday, a weekday lunch offers the dish and dessert of the day.

## Who it's for

For showing someone Brussels in a setting they won't forget, for a family meal (the house describes itself as ideal for families), or for dinner before or after a play at the nearby Théâtres royaux des Galeries: the "before theatre" and "after theatre" service is a house tradition. The maître d', Monsieur Adelin, is happy to tell stories about the celebrities who have dined there, from Charles Aznavour to Line Renaud. The venue is also part of the Brussels Art Deco trail.

## Practical info

- **Opening hours**: Monday to Saturday, from 12pm to 3pm and from 6:30pm to 11:30pm. Closed on Sunday.
- **Weekday lunch**: dish of the day and dessert of the day, €21.90, Monday to Friday.

- **Getting there**: a few minutes' walk from the Grand-Place and the Galeries royales Saint-Hubert; De Brouckère metro (lines 1 and 5) or Brussels-Central station nearby.
