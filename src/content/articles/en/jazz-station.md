On the Chaussée de Louvain, a former red-brick railway station has swapped its trains for concerts. Built on the line linking Brussels-North to the Leopold Quarter, it came into being in 1885, in a mix of Flemish neo-Renaissance and Brabant Baroque. It fell out of use in the 20th century, was listed in 1996 and, once restored, became home to Jazz Station, somewhere between a club and a concert hall. The venue is celebrating its 20th anniversary.

## What to do there

People come above all to listen: the programme regularly features concerts at 8 pm, notably in the "Les Soirs d'Hortense" and "Bruxelles sur scène" series.

Every Sunday at 6 pm it's time for the jam session: musicians take turns on stage, improvising on jazz standards. There's no booking, and everyone pays what they can.

During the day, the venue stays open for its visual art exhibitions linked to jazz (painting, photography, drawing, sculpture), three per season, with free admission.

The youngest have their own events: "Flow" blends dance and musical improvisation for children from 6 months, and the "Zakoustics" concerts are for 0-to-3-year-olds and their parents. Workshops and courses are also on offer, for example in swing dancing, jazz singing or stage presence.

At the counter: coffee roasted in Belgium, teas from a Brussels company, juices and snacks from a Schaerbeek cooperative.

## Who it's for

Jazz lovers and the simply curious. Musicians who fancy getting on stage on a Sunday. Parents of toddlers. And anyone who enjoys Brussels heritage: the building alone is worth a look.

## Practical information

- **Opening hours**: open to the public Wednesday to Saturday, 11 am to 7 pm, and during concerts and events.
- **Prices**: vary by concert, see the calendar on the website. Example from November 2026: €25, €20 for members, students and jobseekers. Optional membership card: €35 per calendar year, with €5 off concerts organised by Jazz Station.
- **Booking**: strongly recommended for concerts, through the online box office. Phone: 02 733 13 78.
- **Getting there**: metro 1 and 5 (Schuman), 2 and 6 (Madou); STIB buses 29, 56, 59, 60, 61, 63 and 64; De Lijn 318, 351 and 526. Parking is difficult in Saint-Josse in the evening.
