On the edge of Avenue Van Volxem in Forest, a large brick building towers over the crossroads. This is the "Blomme", built in 1930 by the architect Adrien Blomme as the flagship of the Wielemans breweries. Since 2007 it has housed WIELS, a contemporary art centre named after a pils once brewed here. Not a museum, not a fine arts palace: a place where you come to see what today's artists are doing, in an industrial setting that by itself tells a chapter of Brussels history.

## What to do here

You visit the **temporary exhibitions** of contemporary art. The programme changes throughout the year: check the website before you come to see what is on.

The building is worth the trip on its own. Renovated between 2005 and 2008, it has kept several remnants of its brewery past. The finest is the **brewhouse**, with its vats, tiles, windows and distinctive light. This is where you will find the **Wiels' CAFÉ**, with a seasonal menu made with products from Brussels producers and merchants, and the **bookshop**, where you can leaf through a book over a coffee.

WIELS also offers **guided tours** for groups, school classes and individual visitors, as well as activities and workshops for children and families. On the first Wednesday of every month, a **late opening** lets you visit until 9 pm.

## Who it's for

For the curious about contemporary art, of course, but also for fans of architecture and industrial heritage. Families will find activities designed for children. The café in the brewhouse is a good stop, even for a short visit. And if you live within one kilometre, the **Welcome Pass** gives you free access to the exhibitions for a year (on presentation of proof of address).
