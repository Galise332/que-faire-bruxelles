At number 9 Rue de Flandre, a stone's throw from Sainte-Catherine, the Roskam states it plainly on its website: "since 1834". Nearly two centuries later, it is still a neighbourhood café, the kind you walk into without a second thought. A long room with a herringbone tiled floor, wooden benches along the walls, small tables, a big bar and a stylised horse as a logo. The name is Dutch, by the way: a "roskam" is a curry comb, the brush you run over a horse's back.

## What to do there

People drop by in the late afternoon, settle in to chat, meet up with friends. But the real appointment at the Roskam is Sunday evening: every Sunday at 8pm, the café hosts a free jazz concert. The programming is carefully curated, with young Belgian bands, groups from Germany or the United Kingdom and musicians well established on the Brussels scene. At the end of November 2026, for example, the bill features the Simon Janssens Quartet and its Running Jokes project, then the quartet hilde. In December, Uranus vs Venus blends free jazz, punk and metal. The full programme is online, with links to listen to the bands before you come.

The Roskam also doubles as a small gallery: since 2019, its walls have regularly hosted exhibitions, photography in particular, including a series on old Brussels.

## Who it's for

Jazz lovers who want to hear musicians up close, without paying an entry fee. Curious visitors who like old cafés and their atmosphere. Residents of the Dansaert and Sainte-Catherine area looking for a simple meeting point. Musicians too: the café accepts concert proposals by email.

## Practical info

- **Opening hours**: Monday, Tuesday and Sunday from 4pm to 1am; Wednesday and Thursday from 4pm to 2am; Friday and Saturday from 4pm to 3am.
- **Concerts**: jazz every Sunday at 8pm, free entry. Programme on the website.
- **Payment**: cash, Bancontact, Visa, Mastercard.
- **Getting there**: Rue de Flandre, a few minutes' walk from Place Sainte-Catherine.
